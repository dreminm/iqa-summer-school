01

Problem

The preceding encoder study asks where pretrained models store quality information. This project starts from one screened CLIP encoder and asks how criterion-specific evidence should be read from its spatial tokens—and whether the answer changes across criteria, layers, instructions, and perceptual priors.

Research question Does conditioning the spatial readout on a criterion produce more accurate and genuinely criterion-specific representations than fixed pooling, task IDs, or language added only after pooling?

Criterion instruction

States what the score means. It is shared across images and should change the queries before they attend to patch tokens.

ARNIQA prior

Provides image-specific distortion-manifold information. It may complement the instruction, but it must beat an ARNIQA-only control and simple late fusion.

Starting hypotheses—not project limits

H1

Task-dependent readout

Instruction-conditioned queries outperform an unconditioned query readout and post-pooling instruction fusion.

H2

Language carries semantics

Full instructions need not beat a task ID on familiar prompts; they should be more stable on unseen paraphrases and CID2013 terminology. Otherwise the model is only task-conditioned, not instruction-driven.

H3

ARNIQA is complementary

A correct image prior improves instruction queries beyond ARNIQA-only and late-fusion controls, especially for noisiness and sharpness, and loses value when matched priors are shuffled.

H4

Criteria use the hierarchy differently

The layer selected for overall MOS may not be optimal for sharpness, noise, color, or contrast. A conditioned readout may benefit from a small set of complementary screened layers.

H5

Queries can specialize

Multiple queries may divide into local artifact, global appearance, and semantic-context roles. Their number, diversity, and interaction may matter more than a larger prediction head.

H6

Shared learning can help or interfere

Related criteria may improve one another, while conflicting score semantics may cause negative transfer. Conditioning should be compared with specialists and ordinary multi-task learning.

Research scope: six human-rated SPAQ criteria provide the empirical center, not the conceptual boundary. Controlled distortions, held-out terminology, layer composition, and new criteria may be explored when they test a stated hypothesis. Open-vocabulary scoring is a possible research question, not a claim granted by the dataset.

First steps

  1. Step 1

    Read the closest work

    CLIP-IQAText controls pooled image–text similarity through antonym prompts; it does not condition a spatial readout. DEIQTA fixed learned quality query over transformer features; the direct readout baseline. ATTIQAUses five quality attributes and text prompts for pseudo-label pretraining, not criterion-conditioned inference. MP-IQECombines visual and language prompts for IQA; use it to state the boundary of the proposed contribution. Qwen3 EmbeddingMotivation for instruction-aware text embeddings. Its retrieval results are not evidence that full instructions will help IQA. ARNIQAThe frozen distortion-manifold prior and its official full-/half-scale embedding path.
  2. Step 2

    Lock the data roles

    SPAQ11,125 authentic smartphone photos; MOS plus five human attribute scores. The only core training set.Official page
    CID2013480 consumer-camera photos; MOS, sharpness, graininess, lightness, and color saturation. Zero-shot attribute transfer only.Official record
    KonIQ-10k10,073 authentic images. Use human MOS only for the overall-quality transfer check.Official page
    LIVE Challenge1,162 authentically distorted images. Overall-quality transfer check; no attribute claim.Official page
    KADID-10k81 references with controlled distortions. Diagnostic stress tests only; type and severity are not human attribute ratings.Official page

    Do not train on KonIQ indicators as human labels: its brightness, colorfulness, contrast, and sharpness files are computed diversity indicators, separate from the subjective MOS file.

  3. Step 3

    Freeze evaluation, not hypotheses

    • Development: one fixed SPAQ split and one seed; results are not reportable.
    • Headline: five approximately 72/8/20 train/validation/test splits grouped by smartphone model, with three training seeds per split.
    • Literature track: five fixed image-random 72/8/20 splits, reported separately because cameras can occur in every partition.
    • Paired-camera track: remove SPAQ's 1,000 repeated-scene images from training, then report within-scene ranking across cameras.
    • Selection: choose epochs and shared hyperparameters by validation macro SRCC. Criterion-specific analysis is allowed, but test labels cannot choose a variant.
    • Transforms: use the screened CLIP transform for every variant; no blur, JPEG, color, contrast, or random-resize augmentation.

    Commit split manifests, prompt lists, target statistics, checkpoint revisions, and the chosen CLIP layer before the main runs.

  4. Step 4

    Build the reference, then investigate

    • Reference: B0 and B1 establish native-pooling and unconditioned-query behavior.
    • Conditioning study: B2–B4 and M1 separate task identity, text content, and conditioning location.
    • Prior study: P0, P1, and M2 separate ARNIQA's predictive value from its role in spatial readout. Use the same image crop for both encoders and the frozen full-/half-scale embedding without its MOS regressor.
    • Research branches: choose layer, query, instruction, task-interaction, or prior experiments from the observed failure modes. More than one branch may be pursued.
    • Scale-up: CLIP-L/14-336 tests capacity. LoRA tests whether adaptation changes the useful layer or the value of conditioning; both are legitimate later research stages.

    Evaluate behavior, not only scores

  6. Step 6

    Report uncertainty and cost

    • Report every criterion, split, and seed; aggregate with median and a 95% group-bootstrap confidence interval over cameras or scenes.
    • Report paired confidence intervals for the primary comparisons and effect sizes per criterion. Distinguish statistical uncertainty from practical value instead of imposing one universal threshold.
    • Measure batch-1 median and p95 latency after warm-up, peak VRAM, parameters, and FLOPs on one fixed device and precision.
    • For M2, count both CLIP and ARNIQA, both ARNIQA scales, preprocessing, and the query head. Report the cached-prompt setting separately from open-prompt text-encoder cost.
04

Expected result

The deliverable is both a compact criterion-conditioned readout and an evidence-backed account of how language, spatial queries, layer choice, task sharing, and perceptual priors interact.

01

Criterion readout

A ≤1M-parameter query module with a simple encode(image, instruction) interface returning an embedding and score.

02

Research map

A structured comparison of layers, query behavior, instruction content, task interaction, and ARNIQA integration.

03

Reproducible benchmark

Split manifests, prompt bank, target mappings, causal interventions, checkpoints, and per-seed predictions.

04

Honest system cost

CLIP-only and CLIP+ARNIQA accuracy–cost points, including the second encoder and prompt-serving assumptions.

Questions the final report must answer

01

What changes the representation?

Separate the effects of learned queries, task identity, language content, conditioning location, and the ARNIQA signal.

02

Where is each criterion represented?

Determine whether one screened layer is sufficient or different criteria require different depths or a multi-layer readout.

03

What do multiple queries learn?

Test whether queries specialize spatially or functionally, and connect that behavior to accuracy and robustness.

04

Is language more than a task identifier?

Use paraphrases, altered definitions, held-out terminology, and criterion transfer—not only in-domain scores.

05

When does ARNIQA help?

Identify criteria, images, and integration points where the prior adds image-specific information, together with its full cost.

06

What generalizes?

Measure transfer across cameras, scenes, datasets, wording, and—where the supervision permits—previously unseen criteria.

Valid negative result

If a task ID matches language, late fusion matches query conditioning, criteria prefer different layers, queries do not specialize, or ARNIQA adds no image-specific gain, explain that boundary. The outcome is a map of what works, what fails, and why—not a requirement to validate one preselected architecture.
